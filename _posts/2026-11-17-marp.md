I am speaking to administrators about how to share my Course Manual with interested students, but in the meantime, I am happy to share my intended reading list. 

There will be seven plenary sessions in Block 4. The readings for the third-to-last session will be determined based on enrolled students' substantive and geographic interests. The last two sessions will be student presentations of their research proposal. (The eighth plenary session for the class will be in Block 5 for students to present their research findings).

The readings for the first four plenary sessions are as follows.

Session 1: Diplomatic Studies and Foreign Policy Analysis 
- Fearon, James. 1998. “Domestic Politics, Foreign Policy, and Theories of International Relations.” Annual Review of Political Science 1:289-313.
- Hudson, Valerie M. 2005. “Foreign Policy Analysis: Actor-Specific Theory and the Ground of International Relations.” Foreign Policy Analysis 1 (1): 1-30. 
- Murray, Stuart, Paul Sharp, Geoffrey Wiseman, David Criekemans, Jan Melissen. 2011. “The Present and Future of Diplomacy and Diplomatic Studies.” International Studies Review 13 (4): 709-728. READ pages 715-716 and 721-725 

OPTIONAL: 
- Elman, Colin. 1996. “Horses for Courses: Why not Neorealist Theories of Foreign Policy?” Security Studies 6(1): 7-53.
- Rose, Gideon. 1998. “Neoclassical Realism and Theories of Foreign Policy.” World Politics 51(1): 144-172.
- Waltz, Kenneth. 1996. “International Politics is Not Foreign Policy.” Security Studies 6(1): 54-57.

Session 2: Studying Foreign Policy Success and Failure
- Baldwin, David A. 2000. “Success and Failure in Foreign Policy.” Annual Review of Political Science 3: 167-182. 
- Kettle, Louise. 2025. “Can Policy Succeed through Inactivity? A Case Study of UK Foreign Policy after the Gulf War.” Foreign Policy Analysis 22 (1). 
- Harvey, Frank. 2011. “Comparative Counterfactual Analysis and the 2003 Iraq War.” In Explaining the Iraq War: Counterfactual Theory, Logic and Evidence, 23-39. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press.

Session 3: Studying the Act of Diplomacy
- Trachtenberg, Marc. 2006. “Developing an Interpretation through Textual Analysis: The 1941 Case.” In The Craft of International History, 79-130. Princeton, NJ: Princeton University Press.
- Nabil, Ahmed. 2024. “Contact Groups as Diplomatic Intervention Tools in Civil Wars: US Diplomacy.” The Hague Journal of Diplomacy 19 (4): 691-722.
- Baudet, Floribert. 2023. “‘Tired of Waking Up on the Floor’: The Temptations and Horror of Cold War Multilateral Diplomacy.” Diplomacy & Statecraft 34 (4): 703-722.

Session 4: Studying When, How, and Why Diplomacy Succeeds or Fails
- de Moraes, Rodrigo Fracalossi. 2025. “Anti-Weapons Activism and Arms Bans: Why Brazil Banned Anti-Personnel Mines but Not Cluster Munitions.” Foreign Policy Analysis 21 (3). 
- Fritzler, Jonas, Caroline Howard Grøn, Anders Wivel. 2025. “Leadership Styles and International Agenda-Setting: Understanding Small-State and Middle-Power Leadership on the Responsibility to Protect.” Foreign Policy Analysis 21 (3).
- Myrick, Rachel and Jeremy M. Weinstein. 2022. “Making Sense of Human Rights Diplomacy: Evidence from a US Campaign to Free Political Prisoners.” International Organization 76 (2): 379-413. 
- Malis, Matt. 2021. “Conflict, Cooperation, and Delegated Diplomacy.” International Organization 75 (4): 1018-1057.
